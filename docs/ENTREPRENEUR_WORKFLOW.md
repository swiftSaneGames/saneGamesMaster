# Entrepreneur workflow

## Question

Every proposed SaneGames title must answer:

> Why should this game exist for the player when other games in the category already exist?

The answer cannot be “because the reference game has downloads.” The Entrepreneur workflow combines reference intelligence, player pain, market evidence, studio capability and original design into a falsifiable product bet.

## Decision funnel

`category evidence -> player feedback -> pain/jobs -> opportunity map -> differentiation -> prototype bet -> measured prototype -> produce/reject`

### Gate 1 — Evidence

A concept needs a source-linked comparison set and explicit unknowns. Popularity, review count, install bands and revenue are different signals.

### Gate 2 — Player problem

At least one meaningful player job, pain or delight opportunity must be stated. Thin evidence stays labelled thin.

### Gate 3 — Original reason to exist

The concept needs at least three meaningful differences from its closest reference. Cosmetic rebranding does not count.

### Gate 4 — Studio fit

The producer checks implementation size, device/test burden, content requirements, maintenance, ad/privacy implications and the existing no-new-spend constraint.

### Gate 5 — Falsifiable prototype

Before expanding production, define what observation would make the studio reject or change the concept. A prototype exists to reduce uncertainty, not to justify a decision already made.

## Opportunity portfolio

Maintain three buckets:

1. **Pain killers** — remove category friction.
2. **Missing jobs** — serve a need existing titles underserve.
3. **Delighters** — original mechanics or feel that create identity.

A strong game can combine all three. Do not let complaint mining produce a joyless backlog of fixes.

## Scoring discipline

Opportunity ranking can use transparent factors such as evidence strength, player value, strategic fit, differentiation and cost. Store the factors beside the score. Run sensitivity analysis and preserve uncertainty.

Market priority remains separate. A cheap, differentiated prototype can be worth testing even when download ranking is unknown. Conversely, a popular category can be rejected when the studio has no credible differentiation.

## Feedback quality

For every analysis report:
- state records analyzed and source mix;
- retain source URL/reference and date;
- distinguish quotes from paraphrases;
- show deduplication/exclusion rules;
- mark single-source or tiny clusters;
- separate competitor reviews, UAT, internal tests and real production players;
- never infer population percentages from an unrepresentative review sample.

## Output contract

The Entrepreneur workflow should hand the studio:
- a one-sentence player problem;
- evidence and limitations;
- top 5-8 opportunities;
- three positioning options;
- recommended thesis;
- cheapest falsifying prototype;
- explicit reject/iterate/produce criteria;
- implications for design, architecture, QA and launch.

The final store message must describe SaneGames' own benefits. Competitor review text is research evidence, never marketing proof.
